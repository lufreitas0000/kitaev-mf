# ADR-001: Algebraic Gauge Projection, Multi-Sublattice Geometry, and PyTorch Array Engine

## Context
Majorana parton mean-field theory (MMFT) on the extended Kitaev model ($J\text{-}K\text{-}\Gamma\text{-}\Gamma'\text{-}D\text{-}\mathbf{h}$) introduces local auxiliary gauge redundancies when fractionalizing each spin-$1/2$ degree of freedom into four Majorana fermions (Kitaev, 2006; Freitas et al., 2024). In the thesis of Freitas (2024), enforcing the local gauge constraints $\langle c_i^T \mathrm{G}^\gamma c_i \rangle = 0$ required an empirical linear adjustment of the Lagrange multipliers. Furthermore, mapping competing collinear and non-coplanar magnetic orders (such as zigzag and stripy phases) alongside inhomogeneous vortex lattices requires a unified geometric abstraction across unit cells (Rau et al., 2014; Winter et al., 2016; Knolle et al., 2018).

## Decisions
1. **Algebraic Rigor & Fixed Kitaev Representation:** Adopt the exact Clifford / CAR $C^*$-algebra Casimir identity $\mathbf{T}_i^2 = \frac{3}{8}(\mathbb{1} - D_i)$, proving that the physical Hilbert space ($D_i = +1$) coincides with the $SU(2)_{\mathrm{gauge}}$ singlet sector $T_i^\gamma |\psi_{\mathrm{phys}}\rangle = 0$ (Fu et al., 2018; Seifert et al., 2018). Fix the spin representation strictly to the Kitaev channel $\sigma_i^\gamma = \frac{i}{2} c_i^T \mathrm{N}^\gamma c_i$ with $\mathrm{N}^\gamma = \frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{M}^\gamma + \mathrm{G}^\gamma)$ (Freitas et al., 2024), avoiding unphysical linear interpolations that violate $(\sigma_i^\gamma)^2 = \mathbb{1}$ on the unprojected Fock space.
2. **General Bond-Directional Exchange Tensor:** Formulate the bilinear spin interaction on directed bonds $\langle ij \rangle_\gamma \in \mathcal{E}$ via the general exchange tensor $\mathbf{J}_{\langle ij \rangle_\gamma}^{\alpha\beta}$ incorporating Heisenberg ($J_\gamma$), Kitaev ($K_\gamma$), symmetric off-diagonal ($\Gamma_\gamma, \Gamma'_\gamma$), and antisymmetric Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya ($D_\gamma$) couplings.
3. **Coupled Root-Finding Solver:** Formulate the self-consistency problem as a unified min-max saddle-point system $\min_{\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{V}} \max_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}} \mathcal{L}(\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{V}, \boldsymbol{\lambda})$ solved via Joint Anderson Acceleration (DIIS) and Augmented Lagrangian (ALM) penalty methods (Yilmaz et al., 2022).
4. **Unit Cell & Graph Convention:** Standardize the lattice spacing to unity ($a = |\mathbf{a}_1| = |\mathbf{a}_2| = 1$, $d_{\mathrm{NN}} = 1/\sqrt{3}$) across the primitive 2-sublattice cell and the 4-sublattice rectangular cell ($\mathbf{A}_1 = \sqrt{3}d_{\mathrm{NN}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}$, $\mathbf{A}_2 = 3d_{\mathrm{NN}}\hat{\mathbf{y}}$), indexing vertices strictly by $i, j \in \mathcal{V}$.
5. **Numerical Stack:** Standardize on PyTorch (`torch.linalg`) in double precision (`float64` / `complex128`) behind the Hexagonal `LinalgBackend` port, ensuring execution parity between local CPU/GPU hardware and cloud accelerators.

## Consequences
- Gauge constraint enforcement becomes automated and mathematically grounded without empirical parameters.
- All unit cells share a unified coordinate scale ($a = 1$) and ultralocal state representation $(\mathrm{U}_{ij}, \mathrm{V}_i, \boldsymbol{\lambda}_i)$.
- The symbol $\eta = J_H / U$ is reserved unambiguously for the microscopic Hund-to-Hubbard ratio in Freitas et al. (2024).
