\subsection{Non-Linear Fixed-Point Solvers on the Grassmannian}
Self-consistency requires solving the fixed-point condition $R(X) \equiv \Phi(X) - X = \mathbf{0}$ on the orthogonal Grassmannian bundle over the Brillouin zone. We compare five solver paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Damped Picard ($\alpha \in (0, 1)$):} Linear contraction $X_{k+1} = (1-\alpha)X_k + \alpha \Phi(X_k)$.
\item \textbf{Joint DIIS (Anderson Acceleration):} Minimal residual subspace combination with relative Tikhonov regularization.
\item \textbf{Good Broyden:} Quasi-Newton secant update $H_{k+1} = H_k + \frac{s_k - H_k y_k}{s_k^T H_k y_k} s_k^T H_k$ with $\mathcal{O}(D^2)$ work per step.
\item \textbf{Limited-Memory Broyden (L-Broyden):} History depth $m=10$, evaluated implicitly in $\mathcal{O}(m^2 D)$ time and $\mathcal{O}(mD)$ storage.
\item \textbf{Jacobian-Free Newton-Krylov (JFNK):} Inexact Newton with GMRES matrix-free directional derivatives.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Real-Space Torus Clusters and Track A Real Skew Arithmetic}
On finite periodic torus clusters of $L \times L$ unit cells ($N_{\mathrm{sites}} = 2L^2$, $M = 8L^2$ Majoranas), we formulate the real skew-symmetric Hamiltonian $\mathcal{H} = \frac{\ii}{4} \mathbf{c}^T \mathbf{A} \mathbf{c}$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Memory Halving:} Real skew-symmetric matrices require $8 M^2 \text{ bytes}$ versus $16 M^2 \text{ bytes}$ for complex Hermitian representations ($10.125\text{ MB}$ vs $20.25\text{ MB}$ at $M=1152$, exact $2.0\times$ reduction).
\item \textbf{Computational Arithmetic and Cache Reality:} Diagonalizing $\mathbf{S} = -\mathbf{A}^2$ via \texttt{dsyevd} consumes $\frac{8}{3} M^3 \text{ FLOPs}$ for the eigensolve itself, but assembling $\mathbf{S} = -\mathbf{A}^2$, forming $\mathbf{S}^{-1/2}$, and multiplying $-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{S}^{-1/2}$ incurs an additional $6 M^3 \text{ FLOPs}$ of matrix multiplications, totaling $\frac{26}{3} M^3 \approx 8.67 M^3 \text{ FLOPs}$ (compared to $\frac{16}{3} M^3 \approx 5.33 M^3$ for direct complex \texttt{zheevd}). Measured wall-clock speedups ($1.36\times\text{--}6.5\times$) arise entirely from continuous cache-line streaming and absence of complex branch overhead in Level-3 BLAS, rather than a net FLOP reduction.
\item \textbf{The $L \equiv 0 \pmod 3$ Dirac Resonance:} On lattices where $3 \mid L$, real-space momentum discretization hits the Dirac point $\mathbf{K} = (\frac{1}{3}, \frac{2}{3})$, causing the smallest singular value of $\mathbf{A}$ to plunge to $s_{\min} \approx 10^{-16}$ and condition number $\kappa(\mathbf{A}) \approx 6 \times 10^{15}$. Squaring $\mathbf{A}$ in Track A squares the condition number to $\kappa(\mathbf{S}) \approx 3.6 \times 10^{31} \gg \epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}^{-1}$, losing half the floating-point mantissa bits and spiking the Frobenius error $\|\Omega_{\mathbb{R}} - \Omega_{\mathbb{C}}\|_F / \sqrt{M}$ from $1.3 \times 10^{-14}$ to $9.5 \times 10^{-9}$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Track B: Eigenvector-Free Matrix Sign Purification and Roofline Scaling}
To bypass both eigensolver diagonalization bottlenecks and condition-number squaring, we evaluate the physical covariance matrix $\Omega = \ii \operatorname{sgn}(\ii \mathbf{A})$ using cubic Newton-Schulz matrix sign purification:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:newton_schulz_formula}
\mathbf{Y}_0 = -\frac{1}{\alpha} \mathbf{A}, \qquad \mathbf{Y}_{k+1} = \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{Y}_k \left( 3\mathbf{I} + \mathbf{Y}_k^2 \right).
\end{equation}
While purification requires $4 k_{\mathrm{iter}} M^3 \text{ FLOPs}$ ($48 M^3$ at $k_{\mathrm{iter}} = 12$, performing $9\times$ more arithmetic than eigensolvers), it achieves unprecedented speedups by operating exclusively in cache-blocked, SIMD-parallelized dense matrix multiplications (\texttt{dgemm}).
Furthermore, because Newton-Schulz is a spectral sign filter rather than matrix inversion, it is completely immune to the $3 \mid L$ Dirac resonance, preserving machine precision ($\|\Omega_{\mathrm{purif}} - \Omega_{\mathbb{C}}\|_F / \sqrt{M} \approx 4 \times 10^{-15}$) across all lattice sizes.

\subsection{Self-Consistent Purification Mechanics and Master Solver Selection}
Embedding Newton-Schulz purification inside an outer self-consistent loop requires three operational safeguards established in Phase~1 closure:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Cold-Reset Spectral Scaling vs.\ Naive Warm-Starting:} Initializing the inner cubic map at outer step $t$ from the previous idempotent projector $\Omega^{(t-1)}$ ($(\Omega^{(t-1)})^2 = -\mathbf{I}$) traps the polynomial in its fixed-point basin and ignores the updated Hamiltonian $\mathbf{A}^{(t)}$. Exact self-consistency requires cold-resetting $\mathbf{Y}_0 = -\mathbf{A}^{(t)}/\|\mathbf{A}^{(t)}\|_F$ at each outer step, while warm-starting the outer vector $X^{(t)}$ via Anderson/Broyden extrapolation reduces total outer iterations from 34 to 9 upon a $\delta h$ continuation step ($3.8\times$ net speedup).
\item \textbf{Damped L-Broyden ($\alpha = 0.5$) for Large Real-Space Manifolds:} At $D = \mathcal{O}(10^4)$, undamped L-Broyden ($\alpha = 1.0$, $m = 10$) diverges due to unregularized unit steps in the $(D - m)$-dimensional orthogonal complement. Damping the base step ($\alpha = 0.5$) restores global contraction ($22$ iterations to $\|R\|_\infty < 10^{-10}$, matching JFNK at $2.2\times$ lower cost).
\item \textbf{Quenched Disorder Conditioning and Gauge Screening:} Under bond disorder ($\Delta K / K = 0.10$) and site vacancies (generating quasi-zero modes $s_{\min} \approx 1.08 \times 10^{-4}$, $\kappa(\mathbf{A}) = 3.82 \times 10^4$), adaptive Newton-Schulz purification converges in $18\text{--}19$ inner iterations with error $6.1 \times 10^{-15}$, while local Lagrange multiplier screening heals inhomogeneous gauge defects over an exponential length $\xi_{\mathrm{gauge}} \approx 1.1 a$.
\end{enumerate}
Summarizing our Phase~1 numerical quantification, \textbf{Joint DIIS ($m=6$)} and \textbf{Full Broyden} are optimal for $k$-space translationally invariant cells ($D \le 500$), whereas \textbf{Damped L-Broyden ($\alpha=0.5, m=10$)} paired with \textbf{Track~B Newton-Schulz purification} defines the production engine for large real-space clusters ($M \ge 512$, $D \ge 10^3$).

\subsection{Real-Space Topological Vortices and In-Gap Majorana Bound States}

Using minimal-length branch cuts on the dual graph, we invert gauge links $u_b \to -u_b$ along a path $\mathcal{P}(p_1 \to p_2)$, producing an isolated vortex pair at $p_1$ and $p_2$ while leaving all bulk plaquettes strictly flux-free ($W_p = +1.000000000000 \pm 10^{-14}$).
