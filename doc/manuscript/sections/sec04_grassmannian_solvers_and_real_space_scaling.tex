\section{Grassmannian Solvers, Pareto Frontiers, and Real-Space Purification}
\label{sec:solvers_and_real_space}

\subsection{Non-Linear Fixed-Point Solvers on the Grassmannian}
Self-consistency requires solving the fixed-point condition $R(X) \equiv \Phi(X) - X = \mathbf{0}$ on the orthogonal Grassmannian bundle over the Brillouin zone. We compare five solver paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Damped Picard ($\alpha \in (0, 1)$):} Linear contraction $X_{k+1} = (1-\alpha)X_k + \alpha \Phi(X_k)$.
\item \textbf{Joint DIIS (Anderson Acceleration):} Minimal residual subspace combination with relative Tikhonov regularization.
\item \textbf{Good Broyden:} Quasi-Newton secant update $H_{k+1} = H_k + \frac{s_k - H_k y_k}{s_k^T H_k y_k} s_k^T H_k$ with $\mathcal{O}(D^2)$ work per step.
\item \textbf{Limited-Memory Broyden (L-Broyden):} History depth $m=10$, evaluated implicitly in $\mathcal{O}(m^2 D)$ time and $\mathcal{O}(mD)$ storage.
\item \textbf{Jacobian-Free Newton-Krylov (JFNK):} Inexact Newton with GMRES matrix-free directional derivatives.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Real-Space Torus Clusters and Track A Real Skew Arithmetic}
On finite torus clusters of $L \times L$ unit cells ($N_{\mathrm{sites}} = 2L^2$, $M = 8L^2$ Majoranas), we eliminate complex arithmetic:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Memory Halving:} Real skew-symmetric matrices require $8 M^2 \text{ bytes}$ versus $16 M^2 \text{ bytes}$ for complex Hermitian representations ($10.125\text{ MB}$ vs $20.25\text{ MB}$ at $M=1152$, exact $2.0\times$ reduction).
\item \textbf{FLOP Reduction:} Real symmetric eigensolves (\texttt{dsyevd}) consume $\frac{4}{3} M^3 \text{ FLOPs}$ compared to $\frac{16}{3} M^3 \text{ FLOPs}$ for complex \texttt{zheevd}, yielding a theoretical $4.0\times$ FLOP reduction and measured wall-clock speedups between $1.36\times$ and $6.5\times$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Track B: Eigenvector-Free Matrix Sign Purification}
Rather than computing full eigenvector matrices $\mathbf{U} \in \mathbb{C}^{M \times M}$, we compute the physical single-particle covariance matrix $\Omega = \ii \operatorname{sgn}(\ii \mathbf{A})$ via cubic Newton-Schulz purification:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:newton_schulz_formula}
\mathbf{Y}_0 = -\frac{1}{\alpha} \mathbf{A}, \qquad \mathbf{Y}_{k+1} = \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{Y}_k \left( 3\mathbf{I} + \mathbf{Y}_k^2 \right).
\end{equation}
