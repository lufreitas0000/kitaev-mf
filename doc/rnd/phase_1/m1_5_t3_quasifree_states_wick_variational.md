# Note 06: Model-Independent Theory of Quasi-Free States, Wick Contraction, and the Variational Grassmannian

## 1. Physical Motivation: A Two-Spin Counterexample to Operator Decoupling

In introductory presentations of mean-field theory, the Hartree–Fock decoupling of quartic operator products into quadratic terms is frequently introduced as an *ad-hoc operator substitution*:
$$A B C D \stackrel{?}{\approx} \langle A B \rangle C D + A B \langle C D \rangle - \langle A C \rangle B D - \dots$$
Such expressions are mathematically false on the operator algebra. 

<!-- claim: CLM-M1-QF-001 -->
**Proposition 1 (Failure of Operator-Level Wick Decoupling on States)**
Consider two spin-$1/2$ particles on a single bond $\langle ij \rangle$ interacting via a ferromagnetic Kitaev exchange $H = -K \sigma_i^z \sigma_j^z$ with $K > 0$. The exact ground-state manifold is spanned by $|\!\uparrow\uparrow\rangle$ and $|\!\downarrow\downarrow\rangle$, in which $\langle \sigma_i^z \sigma_j^z \rangle = +1$.
Representing spins via four Majoranas per site: $\sigma_i^z = -\ii c_i^0 c_i^z$ and $\sigma_j^z = -\ii c_j^0 c_j^z$, the interaction operator is quartic:
$$H = K (c_i^0 c_i^z)(c_j^0 c_j^z).$$
For a generic entangled spin state, the fourth cumulant:
$$\kappa_4(c_i^0, c_i^z, c_j^0, c_j^z) \equiv \langle c_i^0 c_i^z c_j^0 c_j^z \rangle - \left[ \langle c_i^0 c_i^z \rangle \langle c_j^0 c_j^z \rangle - \langle c_i^0 c_j^0 \rangle \langle c_i^z c_j^z \rangle + \langle c_i^0 c_j^z \rangle \langle c_i^z c_j^0 \rangle \right]$$
does not vanish identically. In particular, for the singlet state $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|\!\uparrow\downarrow\rangle - |\!\downarrow\uparrow\rangle)$, $\kappa_4 \neq 0$.

Therefore, **Wick's theorem is not an identity among operators; it is a mathematical property characterizing a restricted family of quantum states**—specifically, the *quasi-free (or Gaussian) states*. Mean-field theory is not an uncontrolled truncation of the Hamiltonian, but the rigorous variational optimization of the energy functional restricted to the quasi-free state manifold.

## 2. Finite Algebraic Quantum Field Theory (AQFT) on Spin Lattices

Let $\Lambda = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$ be a finite graph of $N = |\mathcal{V}|$ sites. 

<!-- claim: CLM-M1-QF-002 -->
**Definition 1 (Quasi-Local Spin Net)**
To each finite region $X \subseteq \mathcal{V}$, associate the finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebra:
$$\mathcal{A}_X \equiv \bigotimes_{i \in X} \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_i) \cong \bigotimes_{i \in X} M_2(\mathbb{C}).$$
The family $\{\mathcal{A}_X\}_{X \subseteq \mathcal{V}}$ satisfies:
1. **Isotony:** If $X \subseteq Y$, then $\mathcal{A}_X \subseteq \mathcal{A}_Y$ via unit injection $A \mapsto A \otimes \Id_{Y \setminus X}$.
2. **Strict Locality (Microcausality):** If $X \cap Y = \emptyset$, then $[A, B] = 0$ for all $A \in \mathcal{A}_X$ and $B \in \mathcal{A}_Y$.
3. **Quasi-Local Algebra:** The total algebra of observables is $\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda} \equiv \mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{V}} \cong M_{2^N}(\mathbb{C})$.

A **state** $\omega$ on $\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}$ is a positive linear functional $\omega: \mathcal{A}_{\Lambda} \to \mathbb{C}$ normalized such that $\omega(\Id) = 1$. The state space $\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda})$ is convex, compact, and finite-dimensional. By the Gelfand–Naimark–Segal (GNS) theorem, every state is represented by a density matrix $\rho \ge 0$, $\Tr(\rho) = 1$, such that $\omega(A) = \Tr(\rho A)$. Pure states correspond to extreme points of $\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda})$.

A local Hamiltonian is defined by an interaction potential $\Phi: \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{V}) \to \mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}$ with $\Phi(X) \in \mathcal{A}_X$ self-adjoint:
$$H = \sum_{X \subseteq \mathcal{V}} \Phi(X).$$
The exact ground-state energy is the convex minimum over all states:
$$E_0 = \min_{\omega \in \mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda})} \omega(H).$$

## 3. Representation Theory: Embedding into the Graded CAR Net

Let $V \cong \mathbb{R}^{2n}$ be a real Euclidean space of dimension $2n = 8N$ equipped with a positive-definite inner product $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$.

<!-- claim: CLM-M1-QF-003 -->
**Definition 2 (Self-Dual CAR Algebra and $\mathbb{Z}_2$-Grading)**
The self-dual Canonical Anticommutation Relations algebra $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{CAR}}(V) \cong \Cl(V)$ is the unital $*$-algebra generated by self-adjoint elements $\{c(f) : f \in V\}$ satisfying:
$$c(f)^\dagger = c(f), \qquad \{c(f), c(g)\} = 2 \langle f, g \rangle \Id.$$
The algebra admits an involutive automorphism $\Theta(c(f)) = -c(f)$ generating a canonical $\mathbb{Z}_2$-grading:
$$\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{CAR}} = \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{CAR}}^+ \oplus \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{CAR}}^-,$$
where even elements commute at spatial separation, whereas odd elements anticommute (**graded locality**):
$$A \in \mathcal{A}_X^-, \quad B \in \mathcal{A}_Y^- \implies \{A, B\} = 0 \quad (X \cap Y = \emptyset).$$

**Local Gauge Structure & Parity Superselection:**
At each site $i$, four Majorana modes generate $\Cl(4)$. The local spin group $\operatorname{Spin}(4) \cong \mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{spin}} \times \mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{gauge}}$ acts on the local Fock space $\mathcal{F}_i \cong \mathbb{C}^4$, decomposing it into:
$$\mathcal{F}_i \cong \left(\tfrac{1}{2}, 0\right) \oplus \left(0, \tfrac{1}{2}\right),$$
split by the local Klein parity operator $\mathcal{D}_i = -c_i^0 c_i^1 c_i^2 c_i^3 = \pm 1$. The physical spin algebra $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{spin}}$ embeds as the gauge-invariant even subalgebra:
$$\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{spin}} \cong P_G \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{CAR}}^+ P_G, \qquad P_G = \prod_{i \in \mathcal{V}} \frac{\Id + \mathcal{D}_i}{2}.$$
Because physical observables are quadratic or quartic even monomials in Majoranas, they commute with $P_G$ and satisfy standard bosonic microcausality despite the underlying fermionic CAR grading.

## 4. Quasi-Free (Gaussian) States and Wick's Theorem

<!-- claim: CLM-M1-QF-004 -->
**Definition 3 (Quasi-Free State and Real Skew Covariance Matrix)**
A state $\omega$ on $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{CAR}}(V)$ is called **quasi-free (or Gaussian)** if for any odd collection of generators, $\omega(c_{a_1} \cdots c_{a_{2k+1}}) = 0$, and for any even collection, the expectation value evaluates to the **Pfaffian** of the two-point covariance:
$$\omega(c_{a_1} c_{a_2} \cdots c_{a_{2k}}) = \operatorname{Pf}\left[ \omega(c_{a_p} c_{a_q}) \right]_{1 \le p < q \le 2k}.$$
The state is uniquely and completely determined by its real skew-symmetric covariance matrix $\Omega \in \mathfrak{so}(2n)$:
$$\omega(c_a c_b) = \delta_{ab}\Id + \ii \Omega_{ab}, \qquad \Omega^T = -\Omega \in \mathbb{R}^{2n \times 2n}.$$

**Theorem 1 (Quantum $N$-Representability of Quasi-Free Covariances)**
A skew-symmetric matrix $\Omega \in \mathfrak{so}(2n)$ corresponds to a valid quantum state $\omega \in \mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{CAR}})$ if and only if:
$$\ii \Omega \le \Id \iff \|\Omega\|_2 \le 1.$$
The state is **pure** if and only if $\Omega$ is an orthogonal complex structure:
$$\Omega^2 = -\Id \iff \operatorname{spec}(\Omega) = \{\pm \ii\}.$$
For a pure quasi-free state, $\operatorname{Pf}(\Omega) = \pm 1$ represents the global fermion parity.

## 5. Differential Geometry of the Fermionic Grassmannian Manifold

<!-- claim: CLM-M1-QF-005 -->
**Definition 4 (The Real Orthogonal Grassmannian $\Gr(n, 2n)$)**
The manifold of pure quasi-free states on $2n$ Majorana modes is isomorphic to the homogeneous space:
$$\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{pure}} \cong \frac{\mathrm{O}(2n)}{\mathrm{U}(n)} \cong \Gr(n, 2n),$$
which has dimension $\dim_{\mathbb{R}} \Gr(n, 2n) = n(n-1) = \frac{1}{2}(2n)(2n-1) - n^2$. It consists of two disconnected topological components characterized by the parity invariant $\operatorname{Pf}(\Omega) \in \{+1, -1\}$.

**Hermitian Projection Representation:**
Each pure covariance $\Omega$ defines an orthogonal projector onto an $n$-dimensional maximal isotropic subspace of $V \otimes \mathbb{C}$:
$$P = \frac{\Id + \ii \Omega}{2}, \qquad P^2 = P = P^\dagger, \quad \Tr(P) = n.$$
The tangent space at $\Omega \in \Gr(n, 2n)$ consists of skew matrices anticommuting with $\Omega$:
$$T_\Omega \Gr(n, 2n) = \{ X \in \mathfrak{so}(2n) : X \Omega + \Omega X = 0 \}.$$

## 6. The Variational Principle on the Grassmannian and Exact Mean-Field Decoupling

Let $H = \frac{\ii}{4}\sum_{a,b} K_{ab} c_a c_b + \frac{1}{16}\sum_{a,b,c,d} V_{abcd} c_a c_b c_c c_d$ be a general many-body Hamiltonian.

<!-- claim: CLM-M1-QF-006 -->
**Theorem 2 (Energy Functional on the Grassmannian)**
For any quasi-free state with covariance $\Omega$, Wick's theorem evaluates the expectation value $\mathcal{E}(\Omega) \equiv \omega_\Omega(H)$ as a quadratic-plus-linear polynomial in $\Omega$:
$$\mathcal{E}(\Omega) = E_0^{\mathrm{core}} - \frac{1}{4} \Tr(K \Omega) - \frac{1}{8} \sum_{a,b,c,d} V_{abcd} (\Omega_{ab}\Omega_{cd} - \Omega_{ac}\Omega_{bd} + \Omega_{ad}\Omega_{bc}).$$

**Theorem 3 (Mean-Field Hamiltonian as Gradient & Euler–Lagrange Stationarity)**
The single-particle mean-field Hamiltonian matrix $h(\Omega) \in \mathfrak{so}(2n)$ is defined as the functional gradient of $\mathcal{E}(\Omega)$:
$$h(\Omega) \equiv 4 \frac{\partial \mathcal{E}}{\partial \Omega} = K + \Sigma(\Omega),$$
where $\Sigma(\Omega)_{ab} = \sum_{c,d} V_{abcd} \Omega_{cd}$ is the Hartree–Fock–Bogoliubov self-energy.
A pure state $\Omega \in \Gr(n, 2n)$ is stationary with respect to variations along the Grassmannian manifold if and only if:
$$[h(\Omega), \Omega] = 0 \iff \Omega = -\operatorname{sgn}(\ii h(\Omega)).$$

**Theorem 4 (Affine Tangent Property & Double-Counting Subtraction)**
Let $H_{\mathrm{MF}}[\Omega] \equiv \frac{\ii}{4}\sum_{a,b} h(\Omega)_{ab} c_a c_b$ be the quadratic mean-field Hamiltonian operator. Then:
$$\omega_{\Omega}(H_{\mathrm{MF}}[\Omega]) = 2 E_{\mathrm{int}}(\Omega) + E_{\mathrm{quad}}(\Omega).$$
Consequently, the true variational energy requires subtracting the double-counted interaction:
$$\mathcal{E}(\Omega) = \omega_\Omega(H_{\mathrm{MF}}[\Omega]) - C[\Omega], \qquad C[\Omega] = E_{\mathrm{int}}(\Omega).$$
Furthermore, $H_{\mathrm{MF}}[\Omega]$ defines an affine tangent plane to the convex energy hypersurface:
$$\mathcal{E}(\Omega') - \left[ \omega_{\Omega'}(H_{\mathrm{MF}}[\Omega]) - C[\Omega] \right] = \mathcal{O}(\|\Omega' - \Omega\|^2) \ge 0.$$

## 7. Quantum Information Diagnostics & State Metrics

<!-- claim: CLM-M1-QF-007 -->
**1. Von Neumann Entropy & Mixedness:**
The von Neumann entropy of a Gaussian state with symplectic eigenvalues $\nu_k \in [0, 1]$ (where $\operatorname{spec}(\Omega) = \{\pm \ii \nu_k\}$) is:
$$S(\omega_\Omega) = \sum_{k=1}^n h\left(\frac{1 + \nu_k}{2}\right), \quad h(p) = -p \log_2 p - (1-p) \log_2(1-p).$$
By Jaynes' maximum entropy principle, a Gaussian state **maximizes** entropy among all quantum states sharing the two-point covariance $\Omega$.

**2. Trace Distance and Uhlmann Fidelity:**
For two pure quasi-free states $\Omega_1, \Omega_2 \in \Gr(n, 2n)$, the Uhlmann fidelity satisfies the closed-form determinant:
$$F(\Omega_1, \Omega_2) = |\langle \Psi_1 | \Psi_2 \rangle|^2 = \det\left(\frac{\Id - \Omega_1 \Omega_2}{2}\right)^{1/2}.$$
The trace distance is bounded by the Bures metric: $D_{\mathrm{tr}}(\Omega_1, \Omega_2) \le \sqrt{1 - F(\Omega_1, \Omega_2)}$.

**3. Non-Gaussianity Relative Entropy:**
For any true many-body ground state $\rho_{\mathrm{exact}}$, its deviation from the quasi-free ansatz is measured by the quantum relative entropy:
$$D_{\mathrm{NG}}(\rho) \equiv S(\rho \| \omega_{\mathrm{G}}[\rho]) = S(\omega_{\mathrm{G}}[\rho]) - S(\rho) \ge 0.$$

## 8. Semidefinite Relaxations (SDP) and 2-RDM Lower Bounds

<!-- claim: CLM-M1-QF-008 -->
While the Grassmannian variational principle yields a strict **upper bound**:
$$E_0 \le \min_{\Omega \in \Gr(n, 2n)} \mathcal{E}(\Omega),$$
the reduced density matrix (RDM) framework yields rigorous **lower bounds** via semidefinite programming (SDP).
Defining the two-body reduced density matrix $\rho^{(2)}$, the fermionic $N$-representability constraints require:
$$\rho^{(2)} \succeq 0 \quad (\text{D-condition}), \qquad Q(\rho^{(2)}) \succeq 0 \quad (\text{Q-condition}), \qquad G(\rho^{(2)}) \succeq 0 \quad (\text{G-condition}).$$
Minimizing $\Tr(H \rho^{(2)})$ subject to these convex LMI constraints produces a lower bound $E_{\mathrm{SDP}}^{(2)}$ sandwiching the true ground energy:
$$E_{\mathrm{SDP}}^{(2)} \le E_0 \le E_{\mathrm{MF}}.$$
The two-site positive semidefinite covariance constraint $\mathcal{C}_{ij} \succeq 0$ proven in Chapter 5 represents the local two-site truncation of this universal hierarchy.
