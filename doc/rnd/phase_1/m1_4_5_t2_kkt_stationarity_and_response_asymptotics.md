# Note 04g: Variational KKT Stationarity, Riemannian Grassmannian Geometry, and Response Asymptotics

## 1. Disambiguation: Global Covariance $\Omega$ vs. Quotient Configuration $\Xi$

**Definition (Global Covariance Matrix $\Omega$)** <!-- claim: CLM-M1-VAR-001 -->
Let $\Lambda = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$ be a finite bipartite lattice graph hosting $N_{\mathrm{sites}} = \vert{}\mathcal{V}\vert{}$ vertices. The real Euclidean vector space generated by four Majorana operators per site is $V = \bigoplus_{i \in \mathcal{V}} V_i \cong \mathbb{R}^{8N_{\mathrm{sites}}}$. A fermionic quasi-free state $\omega$ over the Canonical Anticommutation Relations (CAR) algebra $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{CAR}}(V)$ is uniquely and completely characterized by its real skew-symmetric covariance matrix $\Omega \in \mathfrak{so}(8N_{\mathrm{sites}})$:
$$\omega(c_i^\mu c_j^\nu) = \delta_{ij}\delta^{\mu\nu} + i \Omega_{(i\mu),(j\nu)}, \qquad \Omega^T = -\Omega.$$
Fermionic quantum $N$-representability (density operator positivity $\rho \ge 0$) restricts the spectrum of $\Omega$ to the purely imaginary interval $[-i, +i]$, or equivalently:
$$-\mathbb{1}_{8N_{\mathrm{sites}}} \le \Omega^2 \le 0 \iff \mathbb{1}_{8N_{\mathrm{sites}}} + i \Omega \ge 0.$$
A quasi-free state is pure if and only if $\Omega^2 = -\mathbb{1}_{8N_{\mathrm{sites}}}$. The manifold of pure quasi-free states is the real orthogonal Grassmannian:
$$\mathcal{M} = \{ \Omega \in \mathfrak{so}(8N_{\mathrm{sites}}) : \Omega^2 = -\mathbb{1}_{8N_{\mathrm{sites}}} \} \cong \frac{\mathcal{O}(8N_{\mathrm{sites}})}{U(4N_{\mathrm{sites}})}.$$

**Definition (Unit-Cell Quotient Configuration $\Xi$)**
Under discrete Bravais lattice translation symmetry $\mathbb{Z}^2$, the global covariance matrix $\Omega$ is block-Toeplitz. The physical degrees of freedom quotient into the local configuration $\Xi = (\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{V})$ defined on a single unit cell:
1. **Directed Bond Correlators $\mathrm{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{bonds}} \times 4 \times 4}$:**
   For each directed bond $b = \langle ij \rangle_\gamma \in \mathcal{E}$ connecting source sublattice $s_i$ in cell $\mathbf{0}$ to target sublattice $s_j$ in cell $\Delta\mathbf{R}_b$:
   $$\mathrm{U}_b^{\mu\nu} \equiv \langle i c_{s_i,\mathbf{0}}^\mu c_{s_j,\Delta\mathbf{R}_b}^\nu \rangle_{\mathrm{G}} = \Omega_{(s_i\mathbf{0}\mu), (s_j\Delta\mathbf{R}_b\nu)}.$$
2. **On-Site Correlators $\mathrm{V} \in \mathfrak{so}(4)^{N_{\mathrm{sub}}}$:**
   For each sublattice $s \in \{0, \dots, N_{\mathrm{sub}}-1\}$:
   $$\mathrm{V}_s^{\mu\nu} \equiv \langle i c_{s,\mathbf{0}}^\mu c_{s,\mathbf{0}}^\nu \rangle_{\mathrm{G}} - i\delta^{\mu\nu} = \Omega_{(s\mathbf{0}\mu), (s\mathbf{0}\nu)}.$$
$\Xi = (\mathrm{U}, \mathrm{V})$ is the ultralocal parameterization of $\Omega$. Evaluating single-particle Hamiltonians and spectral projectors in momentum space $\mathcal{H}(\mathbf{k})$ constitutes the discrete Fourier pullback of operators acting on the Grassmannian $\mathcal{M}$.

## 2. Karush–Kuhn–Tucker Stationarity: Grassmannian Saddle Point vs. Convex SDP

**Theorem (Riemannian KKT Stationarity Operator)** <!-- claim: CLM-M1-VAR-002 -->
Majorana mean-field theory solves the min-max variational saddle-point problem:
$$\min_{\Omega \in \mathcal{M}} \max_{\boldsymbol{\lambda} \in \mathbb{R}^{3N_{\mathrm{sites}}}} \mathcal{L}(\Omega, \boldsymbol{\lambda}), \qquad \mathcal{L}(\Omega, \boldsymbol{\lambda}) = E(\Omega) - \frac{1}{4} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{sites}}} \sum_{\alpha=1}^3 \lambda_i^\alpha \operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{G}^\alpha V_i).$$
The first-order Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions on $\mathcal{M} \times \mathbb{R}^{3N_{\mathrm{sites}}}$ require:
1. **Primal Riemannian Gradient Vanishing:**
   $$\mathcal{R}_\Omega \equiv [H_{\mathrm{MF}}(\Omega, \boldsymbol{\lambda}), \Omega] = H_{\mathrm{MF}}\Omega - \Omega H_{\mathrm{MF}} = \mathbf{0}, \quad \text{with } \Omega^2 = -\mathbb{1}.$$
2. **Dual Gauge Residual Vanishing:**
   $$g_i^\alpha \equiv \operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{G}^\alpha V_i) = 0 \quad \forall i \in \mathcal{V}, \; \alpha \in \{1, 2, 3\}.$$

*Proof.*
The tangent space to $\mathcal{M}$ at point $\Omega$ is $T_\Omega \mathcal{M} = \{ \Delta \in \mathfrak{so}(8N) : \{\Omega, \Delta\} = 0 \}$. The Euclidean gradient of $\mathcal{L}$ with respect to $\Omega \in \mathfrak{so}(8N)$ is $\nabla_\Omega \mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{4} H_{\mathrm{MF}}(\Omega, \boldsymbol{\lambda})$. Projecting $\nabla_\Omega \mathcal{L}$ orthogonally onto $T_\Omega \mathcal{M}$ under the canonical Frobenius inner product $\langle A, B \rangle = \operatorname{tr}(A^T B)$ yields the Riemannian gradient:
$$\operatorname{grad}_{\mathcal{M}} \mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \nabla_\Omega \mathcal{L} - \Omega (\nabla_\Omega \mathcal{L}) \Omega \right) = \frac{1}{8} \left( H_{\mathrm{MF}} - \Omega H_{\mathrm{MF}} \Omega \right).$$
Multiplying by $\Omega$ from the right and using $\Omega^2 = -\mathbb{1}$:
$$\left( \operatorname{grad}_{\mathcal{M}} \mathcal{L} \right) \Omega = \frac{1}{8} \left( H_{\mathrm{MF}}\Omega + \Omega H_{\mathrm{MF}} \right) = \mathbf{0} \iff [H_{\mathrm{MF}}, \Omega] = \mathbf{0}.$$
Stationarity with respect to the dual multipliers $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$ yields $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \lambda_i^\alpha} = -\frac{1}{4} \operatorname{tr}(\mathrm{G}^\alpha V_i) = 0$. $\square$

### Comparison with Convex Semidefinite Programming (SDP)
MMFT shares operator cone constraints with SDP, but differs in geometry and convexity:
- **Convex SDP:** Variables belong to the convex cone of positive semi-definite matrices $\mathcal{S}_+ = \{ X : X \ge 0 \}$. Objectives are strictly linear $\min \operatorname{tr}(C X)$ subject to linear affine constraints $\operatorname{tr}(A_k X) = b_k$. Under Slater's constraint qualification, strong duality holds and the duality gap is zero.
- **Majorana Mean-Field Theory:** While representability $\mathbb{1} + i\Omega \ge 0$ is an LMI defining a convex spectrahedron, zero-temperature ground states are restricted to pure states $\Omega^2 = -\mathbb{1}$, which forms the non-convex boundary manifold $\mathcal{M} \cong \mathcal{O}(8N)/U(4N)$.
- **Quadratic Objective:** Due to Wick factorization of exchange bilinears, $E(\Omega) = \frac{1}{4}\operatorname{tr}(h_0 \Omega) + \frac{1}{8}\operatorname{tr}(\Omega \mathcal{K} \Omega)$ is quadratic in $\Omega$.
- **Dual Multipliers:** The concentrated dual functional $E(\boldsymbol{\lambda}) = \min_{\Omega \in \mathcal{M}} \mathcal{L}(\Omega, \boldsymbol{\lambda})$ is strictly concave in $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$ because its Hessian is the negative static gauge susceptibility:
  $$\frac{\partial^2 E(\boldsymbol{\lambda})}{\partial \lambda_s^\alpha \partial \lambda_{s'}^\beta} = -\chi_{s\alpha, s'\beta}^{\mathrm{gauge}}(\omega=0) \le 0.$$
Therefore, MMFT is a non-convex Riemannian min-max saddle-point problem over a Grassmannian manifold.

## 3. Variational Response and Hellmann–Feynman Error Asymptotics

**Theorem (Hellmann–Feynman Master Error Scaling)** <!-- claim: CLM-M1-VAR-003 -->
Let $E^*(\mathbf{h}) = E[\Xi^*(\mathbf{h}), \mathbf{h}]$ be the self-consistent equilibrium energy at external Zeeman field $\mathbf{h}$. The exact analytical derivative is:
$$\frac{\mathrm{d}E^*}{\mathrm{d}h^\gamma} = -\sum_{s=0}^{N_{\mathrm{sub}}-1} \langle \sigma_s^\gamma \rangle_{\mathrm{G}}^* = -\langle \sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^\gamma \rangle_{\mathrm{G}}^*.$$
When evaluating this derivative numerically via central finite differences with step size $\delta$ and solver state convergence threshold $\tau = \max(\Vert{}\Delta\mathrm{U}\Vert{}_\infty, \Vert{}\Delta\mathrm{V}\Vert{}_\infty, \Vert{}\mathbf{g}\Vert{}_\infty)$:
$$\left(\frac{\Delta E}{\Delta h^\gamma}\right)_{\mathrm{num}} \equiv \frac{E_{\mathrm{num}}(\mathbf{h} + \delta \hat{\mathbf{e}}_\gamma) - E_{\mathrm{num}}(\mathbf{h} - \delta \hat{\mathbf{e}}_\gamma)}{2\delta},$$
the total numerical error $\Delta_{\mathrm{HF}}(\delta, \tau) \equiv \left\vert{} \left(\frac{\Delta E}{\Delta h^\gamma}\right)_{\mathrm{num}} - \left( -\langle \sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^\gamma \rangle_{\mathrm{G}}(\Xi_{\mathrm{num}}) \right) \right\vert{}$ satisfies the asymptotic bound:
$$\Delta_{\mathrm{HF}}(\delta, \tau) \le \frac{1}{6} \left\vert{} \frac{\mathrm{d}^3 E^*}{\mathrm{d}(h^\gamma)^3} \right\vert{} \delta^2 + C_\sigma \tau + C_E \frac{\tau^2}{\delta}.$$

*Proof.*
1. Expanding the exact equilibrium functional in a Taylor series about $\mathbf{h}$:
   $$E^*(\mathbf{h} \pm \delta \hat{\mathbf{e}}_\gamma) = E^*(\mathbf{h}) \pm \delta \frac{\mathrm{d}E^*}{\mathrm{d}h^\gamma} + \frac{\delta^2}{2} \frac{\mathrm{d}^2 E^*}{\mathrm{d}(h^\gamma)^2} \pm \frac{\delta^3}{6} \frac{\mathrm{d}^3 E^*}{\mathrm{d}(h^\gamma)^3} + \mathcal{O}(\delta^4).$$
   The central difference isolates the first derivative with $\mathcal{O}(\delta^2)$ truncation error:
   $$\frac{E^*(\mathbf{h} + \delta \hat{\mathbf{e}}_\gamma) - E^*(\mathbf{h} - \delta \hat{\mathbf{e}}_\gamma)}{2\delta} - \frac{\mathrm{d}E^*}{\mathrm{d}h^\gamma} = \frac{\delta^2}{6} \frac{\mathrm{d}^3 E^*}{\mathrm{d}(h^\gamma)^3} + \mathcal{O}(\delta^4).$$
2. At solver termination with tolerance $\tau$, the numerical state error satisfies $\Vert{}\Xi_{\mathrm{num}} - \Xi^*\Vert{} \le C_\Xi \tau$. Because $\Xi^*$ is a stationary point ($\nabla_\Xi E = 0$), linear variations in energy vanish identically. The energy error scales quadratically:
   $$\vert{}E_{\mathrm{num}}(\mathbf{h} \pm \delta \hat{\mathbf{e}}_\gamma) - E^*(\mathbf{h} \pm \delta \hat{\mathbf{e}}_\gamma)\vert{} \le C_E \tau^2.$$
   Dividing by $2\delta$ produces the differentiation noise term $C_E \tau^2 / \delta$.
3. The physical spin observable evaluates to $\langle \sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^\gamma \rangle_{\mathrm{G}}(\Xi_{\mathrm{num}})$. Its error with respect to the true stationary value is linear in the state residual:
   $$\left\vert{} \langle \sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^\gamma \rangle_{\mathrm{G}}(\Xi_{\mathrm{num}}) - \langle \sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^\gamma \rangle_{\mathrm{G}}(\Xi^*) \right\vert{} \le C_\sigma \tau.$$
Combining these three bounds via the triangle inequality proves the claim. $\square$

**Corollary (Quadratic Truncation Scaling Regime)**
When the solver tolerance is chosen such that $\tau \ll \delta^2$ (for example, $\tau = 10^{-8}$ and $\delta = 10^{-3}$), the differentiation noise $\tau^2/\delta \sim 10^{-13}$ and linear bias $C_\sigma \tau \sim 10^{-8}$ are negligible compared to $\delta^2 \sim 10^{-6}$.
In this regime, the numerical error scales strictly quadratically:
$$\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{HF}}(2\delta, \tau)}{\Delta_{\mathrm{HF}}(\delta, \tau)} = 4.0 + \mathcal{O}(\delta^2).$$

## 4. Algorithmic Contraction and Variational Stability

**Proposition (Asymptotic Contraction Rate of Joint DIIS)** <!-- claim: CLM-M1-VAR-004 -->
Let $X = (\operatorname{vec}(\mathrm{U}), \operatorname{vec}(\mathrm{V}), \boldsymbol{\lambda})^T \in \mathbb{R}^D$ be the combined state vector, and $\Phi(X)$ denote the self-consistent map $X^{(n+1)} = \Phi(X^{(n)})$.
During Direct Inversion in the Iterative Subspace (DIIS) acceleration of depth $M$, the iteration residual vector $R_n \equiv \Phi(X^{(n)}) - X^{(n)}$ satisfies asymptotic linear contraction:
$$r_n \equiv \Vert{}R_n\Vert{}_\infty \le C \, \mu^n, \qquad \mu = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{r_{n+1}}{r_n}.$$
The contraction factor $\mu$ characterizes the variational stability of the fixed point:
1. **Gapped Quantum Spin Liquid / Polarized Phase:** The single-particle spectral gap $\Delta_g = \min_{n,\mathbf{k}} \vert{}\mathcal{E}_n(\mathbf{k})\vert{} > 0$ is open. The Fréchet derivative of the spectral projector $\Vert{}D\Pi^-\Vert{}$ is bounded. Contraction is fast:
   $$\mu \in [0.15, 0.40].$$
2. **Gapless Dirac Spin Liquid Phase:** In the pure Kitaev limit at zero magnetic field, Dirac cones touch at $K, K'$ where $\Delta_g \to 0$. Unaccelerated Picard updates oscillate $(\mu \approx 1)$. DIIS dampens the cross-talk between matter and gauge sectors, restoring stable convergence with:
   $$\mu \in [0.65, 0.85].$$
3. **Instability Criterion:** If $\mu \ge 1.0$, the stationary point is dynamically unstable on the current DIIS subspace, signaling entry into a limit cycle or divergence away from an unphysical saddle point.

## 5. Symmetrically Parameterized Klein Duality

**Theorem (Symmetric Klein Invariant Manifold)** <!-- claim: CLM-M1-VAR-005 -->
In the rectangular 4-sublattice cell with sublattices $(A_1, B_1, A_2, B_2)$, the four-sublattice Klein rotation $\mathcal{K}$ maps exchange couplings as:
$$\tilde{J} = -J, \qquad \tilde{K} = K + 2J, \qquad \tilde{\Gamma} = -\Gamma, \qquad \tilde{\Gamma}' = \Gamma'.$$
Fixing the invariant sum $K_0 \equiv K + J = -1.0$, the parameter manifold is parameterized symmetrically by small perturbation vector $\mathbf{\epsilon} = (\epsilon_J, \epsilon_\Gamma, \epsilon_{\Gamma'})^T$:
$$\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{\epsilon}) = \begin{pmatrix} J \\ K \\ \Gamma \\ \Gamma' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \epsilon_J \\ K_0 - \epsilon_J \\ \epsilon_\Gamma \\ \epsilon_{\Gamma'} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \tilde{\mathbf{p}}(\mathbf{\epsilon}) = \begin{pmatrix} \tilde{J} \\ \tilde{K} \\ \tilde{\Gamma} \\ \tilde{\Gamma}' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -\epsilon_J \\ K_0 + \epsilon_J \\ -\epsilon_\Gamma \\ \epsilon_{\Gamma'} \end{pmatrix}.$$
When $\epsilon_J = \epsilon_\Gamma = 0$, the line $\mathbf{p}_0(K_0, \epsilon_{\Gamma'}) = (0, K_0, 0, \epsilon_{\Gamma'})^T$ is self-dual: $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_0 = \mathbf{p}_0$.

### Thermodynamic Invariance vs. Finite-Cell Boundary Splitting
1. **Thermodynamic Limit ($N_{\mathrm{cell}} \to \infty$):**
   At zero external magnetic field ($\mathbf{h} = \mathbf{0}$), the energy density per physical site is strictly invariant under Klein duality:
   $$E_{\mathrm{site}}(\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{\epsilon})) \equiv E_{\mathrm{site}}(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}(\mathbf{\epsilon})).$$
2. **Finite-Size Rectangular Boundary Effect:**
   The 4-sublattice rectangular cell `make_honeycomb_4site_rectangular` wraps periodic boundary conditions along $\mathbf{A}_1$ and $\mathbf{A}_2$. Across the horizontal boundary $\mathbf{A}_1$, bond 3 (an $x$-bond) connects sublattices $2 \to 1$. Because the Klein rotation assigns $(R_{A_1}, R_{B_1}, R_{A_2}, R_{B_2}) = (I, R_x, R_z, R_y)$, the relative rotation along bond 3 inside the cell is $R_x$, whereas wrapping across the supercell boundary $\mathbf{A}_1$ imposes a relative phase twist.
   On a discrete Monkhorst–Pack grid of $N_1 \times N_2$ reciprocal wavevectors, this generates a finite-size energy splitting:
   $$\delta E_{\mathrm{Klein}} \equiv \vert{}E_{\mathrm{site}}(\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{\epsilon})) - E_{\mathrm{site}}(\tilde{\mathbf{p}}(\mathbf{\epsilon}))\vert{} \le C_K \frac{\Vert{}\mathbf{\epsilon}\Vert{}_2}{N_1 N_2} + \mathcal{O}(\Vert{}\mathbf{\epsilon}\Vert{}_2^2).$$
   As $\mathbf{\epsilon} \to \mathbf{0}$ towards the self-dual pure Kitaev manifold, the boundary splitting vanishes continuously.
